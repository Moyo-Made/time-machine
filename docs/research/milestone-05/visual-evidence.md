# Visual evidence assessment

What the images and maps actually show. References such as N-9138069e and C-12 are listed with their addresses in [source-inventory.md](source-inventory.md).

Each entry separates three things:

- **Established:** visible in the image itself.
- **Inferred:** my reasoning from what is visible. It could be wrong.
- **Unknown:** what the image does not settle.

Limits that apply throughout:

- **I viewed preview images**, about 1,400 pixels on the long side for Northwestern's and about 960 for most of the Commons files. Small lettering is often unreadable at that size.
- **Dates on posters are the cataloguer's readings.** I could see the posters but have not confirmed the dates from the previews.
- **Sizes are estimates** from people standing in the pictures. No image has a scale.

## 1. The building in the early photograph

**N-9138069e**, "Casa do Fernandez, on Tinubu Square, Lagos". Print, 164 × 214 mm, DW_12719. Black and white. The catalogue gives no date beyond the collection's span, 1930 to 1972.

**Established**

- The complete street front of a two-storey building, seen almost square-on from open ground.
- Six bays. Each has an arched opening on both floors.
- Each upper bay has its own iron balcony on brackets.
- Pilasters with decorative capitals divide the bays.
- A pierced parapet with urn finials runs along the top. A figure stands on its central pier, with a pole beside it.
- The walls are rendered, with a darker band along the base.
- Signs read "ILOJO HOTEL", "HAMBURGERS DE LUXE / TINUBU SQUARE / BRANCH YABA REST HOUSE" and "OJOBARO BROTHERS".
- The ground in front is open and unpaved. On it are a saloon car, an oil drum, a bicycle and people on foot.
- Utility poles stand in front. Low single-storey structures adjoin on both sides.
- **The bays are not equal.** Measured on the preview, the pilasters fall at about 148, 350, 553, 757, 915, 1,078 and 1,238 pixels. The left three bays are each about 203 pixels wide; the right three about 160.

**Inferred**

- The front is made of two parts of different bay width.
- The photograph was taken after the building began to be called Ilojo, because of the hotel sign. The thesis reports that the name came with the family who bought the building at the end of 1932.
- **It is probably from the later 1940s or the 1950s.** The "ILOJO HOTEL" sign is also in a photograph published in August 1946, and the "OJOBARO BROTHERS" sign is also in one from the 1950s (section 11).
- The car's shape suggests the 1930s or 1940s. This is my judgement and a car can be old when photographed.
- The front is roughly 24 m wide and 12 to 13 m to the top of the parapet, within about 20 per cent either way, judged from the people at its foot. **This estimate is probably too large.** A 1914 deed sketch reproduced in the thesis gives the frontage as 61 ft 1 in, which is 18.6 m; the height would then be about 10 m.
- **The wide bays stand on one plot and the narrow bays on the other.** The deed sketch divides the frontage into 34 ft 4 in and 26 ft 9 in, a ratio of 1.28. The wide and narrow halves of the front measure 1.27 to 1. See [thesis-findings.md](thesis-findings.md).

**Unknown**

- The date, within about four decades.
- The colours of everything.
- The roof, the sides, the back and the depth.
- What the signs' businesses were, beyond their names.

## 2. The building in 2016

Six photographs carry a camera date or a stated one: **C-10, C-11** (17 July), **C-12, C-13** (1 August), **C-17** (7 September by its camera) and **C-01** (stated 7 September; no camera date). Seven more by one contributor, **C-02 to C-08**, carry no date at all.

**Established**

- A two-storey building of six arched bays with an iron balcony at each upper bay, pilasters with decorative capitals, and a pierced parapet with finials and a figure on the central pier.
- Heavy weathering. The render is grey and buff, stained and patched, with plant growth on ledges.
- In the July photographs, red and white tape is strung across the ground floor, and a sign over one door reads "PUBLIC TOILET". Rusted corrugated lean-to roofs are fixed across part of the ground floor.
- Banners hang from several balconies.
- In front: market umbrellas, yellow minibuses, and a yellow sign reading "Welcome to Lagos Island Local Government".
- Shutters on the right-hand upper bays are green. Two upper doors stand open and are blue.
- **A side elevation** (C-05, C-06): two bays deep, with the same arched openings, balconies and parapet. A lower red-brick structure adjoins it.
- **From behind or inside** (C-04): a corrugated metal roof below the level of the parapet, and weathered timber boarding.
- A stair with worn stone or concrete steps (C-03).
- Tall modern buildings stand behind and beside it (C-02, C-05, C-06).

**Inferred**

- The parapet banner is probably one of the monuments commission's. The thesis says the commission hung banners on the building warning that it was protected (page 69), and the banner's last line appears to end "AND MONUMENTS". I could not read the rest.
- **The undated seven were taken between about 1 August and 11 September 2016.** They show a white "PUBLIC NOTICE" banner on the parapet. That banner is in both 7 September photographs and in neither 1 August photograph. This rests on C-17's camera date, and that camera's clock reads 03:45 in daylight, so its date is itself uncertain.
- The roof is a pitched metal roof hidden behind the parapet. C-04 shows part of it only.
- The building is two bays deep along the side seen. The other side and the back are not shown clearly.

**Unknown**

- The plan, at a usable size. A 2012 ground plan reproduced small in the thesis shows an L-shaped outline with no yard.
- The wording of the notice and the banners. In C-01 the parapet banner is headed "PUBLIC NOTICE" and its last line appears to end "AND MONUMENTS". The rest is not legible.
- The inside, apart from one stair and one partial view.

## 3. Is it the same building in both?

**Established.** The early print and the 2016 photographs both show: two storeys; six arched bays on each floor; one bracketed iron balcony per upper bay; pilasters with decorative capitals; a pierced parapet with urn finials; and a figure on the central pier of the parapet.

**Inferred.** They show the same building. The combination is distinctive, the catalogue places the print "on Tinubu Square", and the 2016 photographs are titled and, in two cases, geotagged there.

**The unequal bays are there in 2016 as well.** Measured on two photographs taken from opposite sides of centre:

| Photograph | Taken from | Three left bays, each | Three right bays, each | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-01 | Right of centre, so the right bays look larger than they are | about 263 | about 232 | 1.13 |
| C-12 | Left of centre, so the left bays look larger than they are | about 260 | about 193 | 1.34 |

Widths are in pixels on an enlarged copy. The true ratio lies between the two, which agrees with the 1.27 measured on the square-on print and the 1.28 from the deed sketch.

## 4. Where the building stood: the maps

**M-1911 and M-1913**, viewed at full resolution.

**Established**

- Both print "TINUBU SQUARE" across a triangular open space.
- Both print **"Fernandez"** at the inland point of the triangle, starting at the corner of the block numbered XLVIII.
- The same block is also labelled "Lagos Stores Ltd."
- West of the triangle, block XVII is labelled "Law Courts", with a building outline drawn inside its own enclosure.
- South and south-east of it: "Church", "Tinubu School", "Rhodesian Hotel", "Fire Brigade", "Municipal Office", "St. Anna", "I.T. Palmer" and "E.D. & Co".
- South-west: "Police Constabulary", in block VI.
- The road running into the point of the triangle is labelled "Alli St." on the wider sheet. Bamgbose Street branches east just beyond it.
- **No outline is drawn for the Fernandez premises.** Only the name.

**Inferred**

- The name marks premises at the corner of block XLVIII facing the point of the square. The plan labels other commercial premises the same way.
- Premises known as "Fernandez" existed at that corner by August 1911.

**Unknown**

- Whether the building in the photographs is the one that stood there in 1911. A name on a plan is not a picture of a building.
- The plot's boundaries and the building's footprint.

**M-1962**, viewed at full resolution.

**Established:** it labels Alli Street, Bamgbose Street, Customs Street and Kakawa Street, and "Central Bank" beside the square. It labels a "Supreme Court" far to the east, near the Race Course. It shows no court at the square.

**Unknown:** anything at the scale of a building. At 1:12,500 individual plots are not drawn.

**M-OSM**, retrieved 10 October 2026.

**Established:** a park outline named "Tinubu Square", about 97 m east to west and 51 m north to south, centred near 6.45382° N, 3.38942° E. Nnamdi Azikiwe Street runs along its north side and Broad Street along its south. Alli Street and Bamgboshe Street meet at about 6.45402° N, 3.38997° E, just beyond the east end of the park. No building outline is drawn at that corner.

The reconciliation of these maps is in [geography-and-periods.md](geography-and-periods.md).

## 6. The models

**Established:** handmade card models on a table, with children and young people around them. The court model has a two-storey central block with a domed tower, wings with ornamental crestings, and arcades at ground level.

## 12. The plot one week after the demolition

**W-2016**: ten photographs published on 18 September 2016 by Kọ́lá Túbọ̀sún, with his account of visiting that afternoon. Viewed at about 1,000 pixels wide. In copyright.

**Established**

- An open plot covered in rubble: mostly broken red and orange brick, with reddish earth in heaps, splintered timber and scraps of sheet metal.
- **A fence of corrugated metal sheets on a rough timber frame** round the plot, about twice a person's height. In several pictures men in overalls and hard hats are taking it down, and sheets lie flat on the ground.
- Overalls lettered "LASBCA".
- **A green signboard on two posts**, headed "LAGOS STATE GOVERNMENT", standing in the rubble.
- Along the far edge of the plot: the broken ends of low walls, a small green-painted building, and walls painted yellow and green.
- Behind: a wide, dark slab-like building with a red and white mast beside it, and tall towers, one a bare concrete frame.
- A crowd, television cameras, and a group of men in argument.
- One picture of a cast-iron bracket with scrollwork and a rosette, lying on a floor.

**Inferred**

- **The building's walls were of brick under the render.** The rubble is overwhelmingly brick.
- These pictures show the Casa do Fernandez plot. The account says so, and the dark building and concrete-frame tower behind are consistent with those behind the building in C-02 and C-05. I have not matched them rigorously.
- The bracket is one of the balcony brackets. The account says a resident bought it on site.

**Reported in the account, not visible in the pictures**

- The fence went up after the demolition on 11 September. State building-control staff took it down on the 18th and then put up the sign.
- The demolition took about five hours.

**Unknown**

- What the view from the plot towards the square looked like. No picture clearly faces that way.
- How long the plot stayed in this state. The next dated view is from February 2021.
