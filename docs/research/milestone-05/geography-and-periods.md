# Geography and historical periods

Where the Casa do Fernandez stood, how that was checked, and which periods the evidence supports. Image and map references are listed in [source-inventory.md](source-inventory.md).

## Part 1: where the building stood

### The finding

**The building stood at the north-east end of Tinubu Square, on the corner where Alli Street meets the square, beside the junction with Bamgbose Street.** In present-day coordinates that is about 6.4539° to 6.4540° N, 3.3899° to 3.3900° E, to within roughly 15 m.

This is a plot-level position. **The building's outline is not on any map I found.** An outline does exist, on a 1914 deed sketch and a 2012 measured plan; I have seen both only as small reproductions in the thesis.

### How it was established

Nothing here relies on a written description of the location.

| Step | Evidence | What it gives |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | M-1911 and M-1913 print "Fernandez" at the inland point of the triangular square, at the corner of block XLVIII | The premises' place relative to the square, on a dated survey |
| 2 | The same plans label the road into that point "Alli St.", with Bamgbose Street branching east just beyond | The streets that define the corner |
| 3 | M-1962 labels Alli Street and Bamgbose Street in the same arrangement | The street names and layout persisted |
| 4 | M-OSM shows Alli Street and Bamgboshe Street meeting just beyond the east end of the present park | The same corner in present-day coordinates |
| 5 | C-10 and C-11 carry location data recorded by the phone that took them | An independent check that the 2016 photographs were taken at the square |
| 6 | The 1956 monument notice, as quoted in the thesis (page 54), names the property as 6 Alli Street and 2 Bamgbose Street | The address, from an official document, reported |
| 7 | A photograph published in August 1946 shows a "Bamgbose Street" sign on the corner of the building (thesis, page 51) | Which end of the front is the corner: the left end, seen from the square |

Two cautions on the reconciliation:

- **The old plans are drawn rotated by roughly 30 degrees from north-up.** "The inland point of the triangle" on the plan is the north-east end of the square on a modern map. I matched them by the streets, not by compass direction.
- **I matched by eye.** I did not fit the old plan to modern coordinates mathematically. The 15 m figure is my estimate of how far the match could be off, not a calculated error.

### How this compares with what is written elsewhere

- Wikipedia gives 6°27′14″ N, 3°23′24″ E for the building. That is 6.4539° N, 3.3900° E, which agrees.
- Press reports give the address as Alli Balogun Street and Bamgbose Street. The 1956 notice, as the thesis quotes it, says 6 Alli Street and 2 Bamgbose Street. A 2016 permit quoted in the thesis says 2 Bamgboshe Street and Nnamdi Azikiwe Street, so the two documents name the street on the square side differently. I have not worked out why.
- A plan of the square drawn by Herbert Macaulay in November 1911 is reported to carry the name Couto on the corner plot (thesis, page 40). The survey plans of the same years print "Fernandez". The two fit: the firm was Fernandez & Co, and Couto was the partner living on the corner. I have not seen Macaulay's plan.
- The catalogue's "on Tinubu Square" is right but loose: the building faced the point of the square from across the road, at one end.

### What the maps say about the rest of the square

| Feature | 1911 and 1913 | 1962 | Today (OpenStreetMap) |
|---|---|---|---|
| The open space | A triangle, labelled | Present; too small to read its shape | A park about 97 m by 51 m |
| Law Courts | West side, block XVII, outline drawn in an enclosure | No court at the square. A "Supreme Court" is labelled far to the east | Not present |
| Police | "Police Constabulary", south-west, block VI | Not legible | Not checked |
| Church | South-east of the square | Not legible | A "Church of the Trinity" is photographed at the square (C-37); whether on the same site is not established |
| Central Bank | Not present | Labelled beside the square | Not checked |
| "Fernandez" | Labelled | Not labelled | No outline at the corner |

**The square itself changed shape.** The plans of 1911 and 1913 show a triangle. The present park is closer to a long lozenge with a round fountain at its centre. When and how that happened I have not sourced, so a reconstruction of the open space around 1950 has no dated plan to stand on.

### The plot and its frontage

From the thesis's reproduction of the deed sketches (page 26). The reproduction is small, so the figures are provisional.

- The site was two plots until 1903: Fernandez's, bought in 1890, and the corner plot on Bamgbose Street.
- The 1914 sketch gives the combined frontage to the square as 61 ft 1 in, about 18.6 m: 26 ft 9 in for Fernandez's plot and 34 ft 4 in for the corner plot.
- The side along Bamgbose Street is about 63 ft, about 19 m.
- The built outline in 1914 and in the 2012 plan is L-shaped.

### What is still missing

- **A footprint at a usable size.** The 2012 measured drawings would give it. The deed sketches at full size would give the plot.
- **What adjoined the building on each side** in the mid-century.
- **A mathematical fit** of the old plan to modern coordinates. Worth doing before modelling; it needs four or five street corners common to both.

## Part 2: the periods

I was asked to reconsider the periods, to look at 2016 before the demolition against a verified period after it, alongside the late 1940s, and not to pick a date for the sake of a dramatic shift. Each period is judged against the four gates defined in [README.md](README.md).

### 1946 to the 1950s

"The late 1940s" in Checkpoint 1 was too narrow and too confident. The honest label is a span.

| Gate | Result | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Geographic precision | Pass | Plot fixed on the 1911 and 1913 plans; streets persist to 1962 |
| 2. Dated visual documentation | **Conditional pass for the building.** Pass for walls and street furniture | Three views of the building: one published in August 1946, one from the 1950s, and Northwestern's undated print, which shares a sign with each. I have seen the first two only as small reproductions in the thesis. Four photographs of walls at the square are dated 1948 to 1952 by posters, as the cataloguer read them |
| 3. Reconstructable geometry | Partial | The front is seen square-on and from the corner. A roof shows above the parapet in the 1950s view. Of the other buildings, only Dr Carr's house has a full front |
| 4. Manageable conjecture | Pass for the building and open ground. **Fail for the square** | Facing the building, the player would see an evidenced front. Turning round, almost everything would be invented |

**Honest label for this period:** "between 1946 and the 1950s". Not a single year.

**What makes the pass conditional:** the two dated views must be seen in their original publications before anything is built from them.

### 2016, before 11 September

| Gate | Result | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Geographic precision | Pass | Same plot; two photographs carry location data |
| 2. Dated visual documentation | **Pass** | Five photographs with camera dates between 17 July and 7 September 2016, from four contributors, each showing the building |
| 3. Reconstructable geometry | Mostly pass | Front, one side, storeys and part of the roof are seen, in colour. No footprint; the back is barely seen |
| 4. Manageable conjecture | Pass for the building and its frontage | The market, vehicles and signage in front are photographed. The buildings across the road are not |

**This is the strongest period by a clear margin.** It is also the only one where colour, condition and street life are evidenced.

### One week after the demolition: 18 September 2016

| Gate | Result | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Geographic precision | Pass | Same plot. Tied to it by the account and, loosely, by the buildings behind |
| 2. Dated visual documentation | **Pass** | Ten photographs published on 18 September 2016 with an eyewitness account of that afternoon (W-2016). One photographer, one day |
| 3. Reconstructable geometry | Partly | There is no building to reconstruct. The rubble, the fence, the sign and the back edge of the plot are seen. The plot's outline comes from the deed sketch |
| 4. Manageable conjecture | Conditional | The plot itself is well covered. The view out towards the square is not |

This is a verified post-demolition moment. Its limits are that it is one afternoon, from one source, and that the photographs are in copyright.

### Later: February 2021 to now

| Gate | Result | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 2. Dated visual documentation | **Fail** | One photograph, February 2021 (T-2021): bare ground, tyres, stalls. Nothing dated since. A 2026 article says the state reclaimed the plot and put up a sign, without describing it |

**What the plot looks like now is not evidenced in this review.** I will not describe it. One visit to the corner with a camera would settle it.

### The pairings compared

| Pairing | Evidence for both ends | What the player would learn | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| July to September 2016, and 18 September 2016 | Both ends dated to the day, ten weeks apart | A dated, documented change at one plot, with the evidence for each side in view | **Possible now. The firmest evidence at both ends** |
| 1946 to the 1950s, and 2016 | 2016 strong; the earlier span has three views, two seen only in reproduction | The same building in use and in decay, and how little of the earlier scene can be known | **Possible now**, for the building and its frontage, with the earlier period given as a span |
| 1946 to the 1950s, and after the demolition | Weakest overall | The largest visual contrast | **No.** This is the pairing that would be chosen for drama |
| Any period, and the plot as it is now | Nothing dated for now | | **Not possible yet** |

### What I recommend on periods

1. **Anchor on 2016 before the demolition.** Everything else is compared against it.
2. **On evidence alone, the second period should be 18 September 2016.** It is the only other period dated to the day, and it is the verified post-demolition period the review asked for.
3. **The mid-century span is the better-looking contrast and the weaker evidence.** It can be a later addition for the building itself, with its uncertain date shown to the player as a fact about the evidence.
4. **Leave out the present day** until there are dated photographs.

Two cautions about recommendation 2:

- **Every picture of that afternoon belongs to one photographer.** They can guide a model. They cannot be shown without his permission.
- **It is a recent and contested event**, with living people involved. The game's existing rule fits: it states what is observed, keeps inference weaker than observation, and names no cause or person. A shift between early September and 18 September 2016 can show that the plot changed without saying why.

This is a choice for you. Both pairings pass the gates for the building's plot; they differ in how firm the dates are and in what the game would be about.
