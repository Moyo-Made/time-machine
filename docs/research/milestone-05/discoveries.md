# Defensible historical discoveries

Things a player could find at this site that the evidence in this review supports. This is a list of what could be said, not a script. Nothing here has been added to the game.

Each entry keeps the game's existing separation:

- **Observed:** what the source itself shows.
- **Inferred:** a conclusion drawn from it, never stronger than the observation.
- **Unknown:** what stays open.

No entry names a cause. References are listed in [source-inventory.md](source-inventory.md).

## Ready: the evidence was viewed and is dated or datable

### 1. A name on the plan

- **Observed:** survey plans of Lagos revised to August 1911 and January 1913 print the word "Fernandez" at one point of the triangular Tinubu Square.
- **Inferred:** premises known by that name stood at this corner by 1911.
- **Unknown:** what the premises looked like then. The plan draws no building.
- **Source:** M-1911, M-1913. Public domain; the plans themselves could be shown.

### 2. A square that was a triangle

- **Observed:** the 1911 and 1913 plans draw Tinubu Square as a triangle. Photographs from 2014 to 2024 show a fenced park with a central fountain.
- **Inferred:** the open space was reshaped at some time between.
- **Unknown:** when, and in how many stages.
- **Source:** M-1911, M-1913, C-00, C-14, C-33.

### 3. The court that is on one map and not the next

- **Observed:** the 1913 plan draws "Law Courts" in an enclosure on the west side of the square. The 1962 map shows no court there and labels a "Supreme Court" elsewhere.
- **Inferred:** the court's site at the square went out of that use between 1913 and 1962.
- **Unknown:** the year. The catalogue says the building was demolished in 1960 and gives no source.
- **Source:** M-1913, M-1962; catalogue note on N-9c2e511d.

### 4. The building in its last summer

- **Observed:** photographs taken between 17 July and 7 September 2016 show a two-storey building of six arched bays, weathered and stained, with tape across its ground floor in July, a "PUBLIC TOILET" sign over one door, and a market and minibuses in front.
- **Inferred:** by mid-2016 it was in poor repair and its ground floor was in makeshift use.
- **Unknown:** who occupied it, and what the notice hung on its parapet said.
- **Source:** C-10 to C-13, C-17. CC BY-SA 4.0; could be shown with attribution.

### 5. A notice appears

- **Observed:** photographs of 1 August 2016 show no banner on the parapet. Photographs dated 7 September 2016 show a white banner headed "PUBLIC NOTICE".
- **Inferred:** the notice went up in the five weeks between.
- **Unknown:** its full wording and who put it there. Its last line appears to end "AND MONUMENTS". One of the two September dates comes from a camera whose clock was wrong by hours.
- **Source:** C-12, C-13, C-17, C-01.

### 6. Dating a photograph from a poster

- **Observed:** photographs of walls at the square include posters. The cataloguer read dates on four of them: 8 October 1948, 8 November 1948, 2 May 1952 and 29 June 1952.
- **Inferred:** those photographs were taken around those dates, and the series was made over several years.
- **Unknown:** how long each poster had been up.
- **Source:** N-68c9c9b6, N-5756dc04, N-6778adc8, N-8f094eb3. In copyright; describe, do not show.
- **Why it is worth including:** it teaches how an undated picture is dated, which is the game's subject.

## Ready, with the date stated as uncertain

### 7. Three signs on an old front

- **Observed:** an undated print shows the building with signs reading "ILOJO HOTEL", "HAMBURGERS DE LUXE / TINUBU SQUARE / BRANCH YABA REST HOUSE" and "OJOBARO BROTHERS".
- **Inferred:** when the photograph was taken, the building housed a hotel and at least one other business.
- **Unknown:** the year. The same signs appear in photographs of 1946 and the 1950s, so probably within that span.
- **Source:** N-9138069e. In copyright; describe, do not show.

### 8. The same building, twice

- **Observed:** the undated print and the 2016 photographs both show six arched bays on two floors, a bracketed balcony at each upper bay, pilasters with decorative capitals, and a pierced parapet with a figure on its central pier.
- **Inferred:** they show the same building.
- **Unknown:** what was altered between the two.
- **Source:** N-9138069e; C-10 to C-13.

### 9. Bays that do not match

- **Observed:** in the undated print, the three left-hand bays are each wider than the three right-hand bays, by about a quarter. Two photographs from 2016, taken from opposite sides, show the same difference.
- **Observed, in a document reported by the thesis:** a deed sketch of 1914 divides the frontage into two parts, 34 ft 4 in and 26 ft 9 in. That is also a difference of about a quarter.
- **Inferred:** the front stands on two plots, and the change in bay width marks where they meet.
- **Unknown:** when the two parts were joined behind one front. The thesis puts it between 1903 and 1914.
- **Source:** N-9138069e; C-01, C-12; thesis, pages 26 to 28.
- **Why it is worth including:** the player can see the evidence for themselves, in the building's own front.

### 10. A house with a bookshop sign

- **Observed:** an undated photograph shows a two-storey house with a hipped roof, dormers and a columned porch, carrying a sign for the British Council's books department.
- **Inferred:** at the time, the British Council used the house.
- **Unknown:** the year, and exactly where on the square it stood. The catalogue identifies it as Dr Henry Carr's former house at number 7.
- **Source:** N-bfe6a5a3, N-67ad2401.

## Usable as statements attributed to the thesis

Each of these rests on a document the thesis cites and I have not seen. In the game they would be worded as what the research found, with the thesis named.

### 11. Two plots, two owners

- **Reported:** land deeds record that a trader named José Amoedo Fernandes bought one plot on the square in 1890, that a second trader bought the corner plot beside it in 1895, and that Fernandez owned both only from May 1903.
- **Inferred, by the thesis:** the building as it was later known was made between 1903 and 1914.
- **Unknown:** the year. No document found states one.
- **Source:** thesis, pages 25 to 28, citing five deeds in the Lagos Land Registry.

### 12. A merchant from Galicia

- **Reported:** the 1914 deed describes Fernandez as a merchant of a village in Pontevedra, in Spain.
- **Inferred:** the building's first owner was not a returnee from Brazil, as was long said.
- **Unknown:** who designed and built it. No record names them.
- **Source:** thesis, pages 29 and 44.

### 13. A history that was contested

- **What can be said:** for decades the building was dated to 1846, 1855 or about 1880. A 2021 study traced each of those dates to a single interview, an untraceable book, or no source at all, and went to the land deeds instead.
- **Why it is worth including:** it is a real case of repeated history and archival research disagreeing, at the very building the game would reconstruct. It teaches the game's subject: how do we know?
- **Source:** thesis, pages 22 to 28 and 59.

### 14. A monument notice

- **Reported:** on 5 April 1956 a notice in the official gazette declared the building known as "Ilojo Bar", at 6 Alli Street and 2 Bamgbose Street, a monument.
- **Source:** thesis, page 54, citing Legal Notice 31 of 1956.

### 15. The measuring of 2012

- **Reported:** early in 2012 the front was scaffolded and the building measured in detail by a heritage group and an architects' practice.
- **Inferred:** a full record of the building's dimensions exists.
- **Source:** thesis, pages 63 and 64, with photographs of the survey.

### 16. The figure on the parapet

- **Observed:** a figure stands on the central pier of the parapet in every photograph, from 1946 to 2016.
- **Reported:** it was a ceramic figure of Spring from a workshop in Porto, made between 1884 and 1905, identified from lettering on its base photographed in 2012. People called it the Angel.
- **Source:** thesis, pages 73 and 74.

### 17. The demolition

- **What can be said:** the building was demolished on 11 September 2016.
- **What cannot be said:** who was responsible or why. That is disputed, and the game does not name causes.
- **Source:** thesis, pages 69 to 71; the eyewitness account of 18 September 2016.

## Ready, from the plot after the demolition

### 18. Brick under the render

- **Observed:** photographs taken on 18 September 2016 show the plot covered in broken red brick.
- **Inferred:** brick was the main building material on the plot. Some of it may come from the lower range at the back, which was bare brick; so this does not by itself show what was under the front's render.
- **Unknown:** what the front's walls were made of; where the brick was made.
- **Source:** W-2016. In copyright; describe, do not show.

### 19. A fence, then a sign

- **Observed:** on 18 September 2016 a fence of corrugated metal sheets stood round the plot and was being taken down. A green signboard headed "Lagos State Government" was then standing in the rubble.
- **Inferred:** the plot's status was being asserted in public that day.
- **Unknown:** what happened to the plot afterwards. A photograph from February 2021 shows it as open ground; nothing dated is later.
- **Source:** W-2016; thesis, page 71.

## Not usable

| Claim | Why not |
|---|---|
| Built in 1846, 1855 or about 1880 | Each traced by the thesis to an unsupported origin |
| A single construction year of any kind | The best evidence gives a range, by inference |
| Built or owned by returnees from Brazil, as a fact | Ownership is contradicted by the deeds. Who built it is undocumented; the thesis's view that returnee craftsmen did is an inference |
| Any connection of the building with the slave trade | The thesis finds it impossible for this building and unconfirmed for the land before it. Too serious a claim to make on that footing |
| Who was responsible for the demolition | Disputed. Not for the game |
| The fountain was a gift from the Lebanese community | I found no source at all |
| The Supreme Court building dated from 1918, or a first court from 1869 | Three weak sources, not reconciled |
| The shape of the court or of the square, taken from the card models | A model of unknown date and purpose is not a record |
| Anything about the plot after February 2021 | No dated evidence |

## How many are supportable

Twelve discoveries rest on evidence I viewed. Eight of those are dated or datable; four depend on photographs whose date is a span and must say so. Seven more can be used as statements attributed to the thesis. The fictional prototype has three points of interest and one connection, so the site can carry more content than the prototype does now, without invention.
